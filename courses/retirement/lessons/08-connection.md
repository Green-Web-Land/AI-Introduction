# 8. Draft an invitation while keeping choice human

[Previous](07-messages.md) · [Course home](../README.md) · [Next](09-control.md)

## Objective

Revise wording without inventing commitments or pressuring a recipient.

## Explanation

A writing tool does not know the relationship or another person's wishes. Decide what you mean before drafting. Leave room to decline. You decide whether to send; this course sends nothing.

Editing by hand is equally valid. Warmth does not require invented attendance, facilities, promises or feelings. AI wording is a draft, not a substitute for listening to a real response.

## Worked example

Brief: Jo wants to ask Pat about a possible sketch afternoon. No date, venue or transport agreed; Pat may decline.

Bad draft: Everyone is coming Friday, I booked the room and you must join us.

Revision: Would you be interested in a sketch afternoon sometime? We have not chosen a date or place. No pressure if it is not for you.

## Practice

Facts: two people are considering a reading circle; no book or date chosen; recipient prefers brief messages.

Draft: Our weekly circle starts tomorrow. We bought your book and expect you there.

Identify invented commitments. Rewrite in at most three sentences with a way to decline.

## Answers and checking

Weekly frequency, tomorrow's start, a purchased book and expected attendance are invented. Example: We are considering a reading circle and have not chosen a book or date. Would you be interested? It is fine to say no. Other fact-preserving, non-pressuring versions work. Do not send the exercise to a real person.

## Try independently

Imagine a decline. Write a respectful one-sentence acknowledgment without trying to change the person’s mind. Consider whether a tool is needed.
