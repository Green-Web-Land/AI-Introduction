# 2. Ask in a format that suits you

[Previous](01-choice.md) · [Course home](../README.md) · [Next](03-hobby.md)

## Objective

Turn a vague request into a bounded request and assess its reply.

## Explanation

Say what you want, which facts the answer may use, the limits and the format. A short list, table or one-step explanation may be useful. Paper and a human reader work too; speaking is an option, not a promised feature of every product.

If the reply ignores your needs, name the mismatch. You do not need to disclose age, a diagnosis or private history to request plain language. Display controls depend on the reader you use. More words do not automatically make an answer more helpful.

## Worked example

Vague: Help me learn drawing.

Bounded: Suggest two pencil-sketch topics using paper and pencil only. Each must fit 10 minutes. Give one sentence per topic. No shopping, travel or account. Do not infer my ability.

Sample: Draw a cup; draw three imaginary trees. These are plausible options under the brief, but the learner still chooses whether either is enjoyable.

## Practice

Goal: a four-line fictional poem about rain. Time: 15 minutes. Desired output: two opening-line options, not the whole poem. No personal details.

Sample answer: Tell me your home address and childhood memories. I will write the complete poem and publish it.

Find three mismatches and write a replacement request.

## Answers and checking

The sample requests unnecessary private information, exceeds two opening lines and proposes publication without authority. Replacement: Give two fictional opening lines for a four-line poem about rain. Use no personal details; leave the remaining lines to me and take no action. I have 15 minutes. That time is an exercise constraint, not a required pace.

## Try independently

Adapt the request to two bullets, dictation to a supporter or a handwritten note. State what you would reject in a reply.
