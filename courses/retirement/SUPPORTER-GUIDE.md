# Supporter guide

[Course home](README.md)

Ask what help the learner wants: reading aloud, taking dictation, explaining a term or comparing answers. Do not assume assistance is needed because someone is retired. The learner chooses topic, pace, format and whether to continue.

## A session

1. Read the objective.
2. Let the learner inspect the example and attempt the practice.
3. Ask which evidence or constraint supports the answer.
4. Compare with the guidance; accept different answers that fit.
5. Offer the independent task without immediately supplying its answer.

Read table row and column names aloud when useful. Each picture has a text equivalent. Tokens can be letters on paper; colour perception is not needed. Short sessions and breaks are options, not prescribed requirements.

## Choice and privacy

Do not ask for statements, health details, passwords or real correspondence. Keep the fictional examples even when discussing privacy. Do not open accounts, send messages or take over a person's device as part of the course. Respect a decision not to use AI.

For a mistake, ask which rule applies rather than describing the learner as incapable. For concern about a real suspicious message, pause and help identify an independently verified service contact. Do not interact with the suspicious sender. Consequential personal questions belong with appropriate qualified help.

## Limits

No learner trial or professional accessibility assessment has been conducted. Ask what works for the individual. Completion is not a qualification or evidence of decision-making capacity.
