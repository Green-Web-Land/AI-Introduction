# Check claims and media

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Objective:** Separate a circulating claim from evidence that supports it.

A screenshot, voice clip or confident paragraph can be misleading, edited, invented or missing context. Appearance alone does not establish provenance: where it came from and what happened to it. A claim of “AI-generated” is also something to verify, not an automatic verdict.

Fictional example: a cropped screenshot says a youth centre cancelled every weekend activity. It shows no date, author or original link. Someone asks you to repost it immediately.

## Practise

Write three checks before sharing. Possible answers:
- Look for the original announcement and date.
- Check the centre's own current information through a known route.
- Compare the actual scope: one event, one weekend or every activity?

For this paper exercise, the supplied original notice reads: “Saturday's art workshop is postponed. Other activities continue.” The screenshot's broad claim is unsupported.

Draft a correction without accusing an unknown person of deliberate deception. Example: “The original notice concerns Saturday's art workshop, not all weekend activities.”

## Check yourself

A second chatbot repeats the screenshot. Is that independent confirmation? Not necessarily: it may use the same source or invent an answer. Ask what evidence supports the claim.

Do not upload private or disturbing material to investigate it. If content targets or threatens someone, stop circulation and ask a trusted adult for help using an appropriate reporting route.

**Transfer task:** list what you would need to verify an impressive statistic: original source, date, population, method and what the number actually measures.

**Takeaway:** pause before amplification. Sometimes the responsible result is “not verified.”

All situations, outputs and worked examples in this lesson are fictional teaching material. No live AI account or submission is required.
