# Ask clearly and refine

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Objective:** Turn a vague request into a bounded task, then check the result.

A helpful request describes the goal, supplied facts, limits and desired output. It can also say what is unknown. These instructions improve clarity; they do not enforce privacy or guarantee compliance.

Fictional brief: a reading club needs two quiet activities for six people, using only paper and pencils, lasting thirty minutes total. No real names or locations are needed.

Possible request: “Using these facts, suggest two activities totalling thirty minutes. Give materials and steps. Do not invent participant information. Flag missing facts that affect the plan.”

A pretend answer proposes a twenty-minute discussion and a twenty-minute drawing activity. It sounds useful, but forty minutes breaks the limit.

## Practise

Write a correction request that identifies the error without asking for a complete unrelated rewrite. Then draft your own corrected plan.

One answer: “Your activities total forty minutes. Revise them to thirty minutes total and retain the materials limit.” A fifteen-minute discussion and fifteen-minute drawing activity fits the time constraint. Whether the activities suit participants still needs a human decision.

## Check yourself

What if the organiser has not said whether participants need accessible formats? Do not invent “everyone can read small print.” Flag the missing requirement and ask the responsible adult or organiser in an appropriate way.

**Transfer task:** write a request for three titles for an invented article. Specify audience, maximum length and tone. Check a title yourself before using it.

**Takeaway:** good prompting includes checking. Giving an instruction is different from proving it was followed.

All situations, outputs and worked examples in this lesson are fictional teaching material. No live AI account or submission is required.
