# Apply principles in a final project

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Objective:** Design a useful bounded AI task and explain its checks and responsibilities.

Our proposed principles are to expand human capability, preserve choice, protect privacy, treat people fairly, explain important outputs and make mistakes open to challenge. They are proposals, not a worldwide legal code.

A law, a product's rules and a technical safeguard are different things. “Respect privacy” expresses a principle; limiting what data an application collects is a practical step. A prompt alone does not implement that limit.

## Final project

Design a fictional assistant for a community book exchange. Keep all people, details and outputs invented. Work on paper or in a local document.

Produce:
1. A useful task and one reason AI might help.
2. A non-AI alternative.
3. A request using only necessary fictional facts.
4. A pretend output, labelled invented, containing one mistake.
5. An explanation of how to find and correct that mistake.
6. A rule about what the assistant may not do.
7. An accessibility or fairness improvement.
8. A human review and correction route.

## Worked outline

Task: suggest categories for ten supplied fictional books. Alternative: sort them manually. Mistake: a category omits a book. Check: compare every input with the output. Boundary: never contact people or collect real reading histories. Accessibility: provide readable text and a linear alternative to a table. Correction: the organiser can revise categories and explain the change.

## Check your project

Can someone else follow the task and reproduce your check? Did you distinguish suggestions from facts and actions? Is the non-AI route workable? Is someone responsible when a result is wrong? Revise any missing element.

Completing this activity is practice, not a qualification or proof of tested competence.

**Transfer task:** adapt your design to a different fictional project and identify what changes.

**Takeaway:** a good AI workflow includes human choices, evidence and a way to correct mistakes.

All situations, outputs and worked examples in this lesson are fictional teaching material. No live AI account or submission is required.
