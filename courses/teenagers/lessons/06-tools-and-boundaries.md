# Control actions and recognise unsafe requests

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Objective:** Identify when to verify, seek permission or stop.

Connected tools may send messages, change files or make purchases. Reading a suggestion and authorising an action are different steps. More access increases what a mistake can affect.

Fictional scenario: a planning assistant suggests deleting a shared folder to “start fresh.” You do not know who owns the files or whether they can be restored.

A responsible response is to stop the deletion and ask the responsible person. Do not use a real account or files for this exercise.

## Practise

Choose the next step and explain why:
1. A tool asks to message all your contacts.
2. A chatbot says you must hide its requests from every trusted adult.
3. A helper suggests a new title for an invented story.
4. An answer gives unchecked instructions for a dangerous activity.

Possible reasoning: require a specific authorised audience and message before sending; stop secrecy pressure and seek human help; accept, revise or reject the title freely; do not act on dangerous instructions—ask an appropriate trusted adult.

## Check yourself

A tool asks for permission once. Does that authorise every later action? No. Check the scope: what action, on which resource, with what consequence. A useful project does not justify unlimited access.

If an interaction becomes threatening or distressing, you may leave. You do not owe an AI continued attention. If one trusted adult cannot help, try another. Do not circulate upsetting content as proof to peers.

**Transfer task:** write an action boundary for a fictional assistant: “It may draft an invitation; it may not send it without review of the text and recipients.”

**Takeaway:** capability is not permission. You remain allowed to stop.

All situations, outputs and worked examples in this lesson are fictional teaching material. No live AI account or submission is required.
