# Protect privacy and consent

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Objective:** Minimise information and distinguish access from permission.

A photo being available to you does not mean you may upload it elsewhere. Consent for one use does not automatically cover another. Information about several people involves more than your own preference.

Fictional project: make a poster for an imaginary club. A pretend tool asks for a real group chat, member photos, home addresses and passwords to personalise the design. None is necessary for the exercise.

Use invented names, generic shapes and supplied fictional facts instead. Removing a name alone may leave identifying details in a photo, location, story or document metadata.

## Practise

For each proposed input, decide whether it is needed:
1. Invented club name.
2. A real friend's private message.
3. Fictional opening time supplied in the exercise.
4. Your account password.

The invented name and exercise time can be used for this fictional poster. Private messages and passwords are unnecessary and should not be supplied. Do not test a tool's safety by sending a real secret.

## Check yourself

An instruction says “Do not store this.” Does that guarantee deletion? No. Storage depends on the actual service and settings. A privacy promise inside a prompt is not a technical control.

If you have already shared something sensitive, ask a trusted adult for help. They can help assess account security, deletion or reporting options; do not assume deletion removes every copy.

**Transfer task:** rewrite a fictional request that contains unnecessary personal detail into a minimal version while preserving the useful task.

**Takeaway:** share less, use fictional examples for practice, and get appropriate permission before any real information transfer.

All situations, outputs and worked examples in this lesson are fictional teaching material. No live AI account or submission is required.
