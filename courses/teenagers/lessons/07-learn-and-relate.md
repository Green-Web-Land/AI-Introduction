# Learn and keep human relationships

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Objective:** Use assistance without handing over your learning or judgment about people.

An AI system can produce patient-sounding responses. That does not establish that it knows you, cares for you as a person, or can reliably judge someone else's motives. It should not become the authority on your relationships.

Fictional example: Sam's friend replies “later” to a message. A chatbot says, “They definitely dislike you.” There is not enough evidence for that conclusion. They may be busy, distracted or mean something else.

## Practise

Write a respectful clarification in Sam's own voice. One possibility: “No rush—let me know when you have time.” Other respectful answers can work. Do not upload real private messages to complete this exercise.

Now practise learning: the fictional sequence is 3, 6, 9, 12. Try the next number before reading a hint. Hint: compare the gaps. Under the add-three rule, fifteen comes next. Explain the rule, then make a new sequence yourself.

## Check yourself

Did a hint help you solve a new task, or did you only copy an answer? Test your understanding on a different example without assistance. A short sequence can fit multiple patterns; explain your chosen rule.

For relationships, AI can help rehearse wording, but cannot provide consent on another person's behalf or replace a direct conversation. Never use it for covert monitoring, manipulation or diagnosing a friend.

**Transfer task:** identify one situation where you would choose a conversation, a reference book or independent practice instead of AI.

**Takeaway:** useful assistance leaves room for your voice, your effort and other people's choices.

All situations, outputs and worked examples in this lesson are fictional teaching material. No live AI account or submission is required.
