# Facilitator guide

[Home](README.md) · [Curriculum](CURRICULUM.md)

For parents, educators and youth facilitators. Preview each lesson and adapt the pace, language and format without assuming every teenager has the same abilities, interests or access.

## Facilitation

Ask for the learner's reasoning before showing the worked answer. Let them disagree with creative suggestions, identify unknowns and choose ordinary tools. Offer spoken, written or drawn responses; provide readable text and a linear view of tables. No score or submission is required. Respect a choice to pause or avoid a topic.

Use the final project's eight outputs as discussion prompts. A missing verification step needs revision, not a judgment of the learner's worth. Ask for a new example to distinguish understanding from copying.

## Privacy and optional demonstrations

Every core exercise is offline and fictional. Do not ask participants to upload private messages, schoolwork, identifying stories, photos or voices, or to create accounts for the course. Do not collect learner answers through repository issues. Use adult feedback with invented examples.

Before an optional live demonstration, check the specific service's current age eligibility, permission requirements, data practices and institutional rules. Supervision does not override terms. Use synthetic inputs and preview outputs. No service is endorsed; no particular tool is necessary.

## Distress and relationships

Do not invite personal disclosures to make exercises realistic. If a learner is upset, stop the activity, listen without blame and use an appropriate trusted-adult or safeguarding route. They need not reproduce disturbing content. AI is not a mental-health assessment or an authority on another person's feelings. Do not turn the relationship exercise into advice about a learner's actual private situation.

## Evidence limits

This edition has author checks, not validated learning outcomes or professional legal certification. A learner pilot would need a separate approved plan for consent, safeguarding, data minimisation and reporting. Do not describe classroom use or completion as proof of effectiveness.
