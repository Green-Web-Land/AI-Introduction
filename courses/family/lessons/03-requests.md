# 3. Make a clear request in your own voice

[Previous](02-facts.md) · [Course home](../README.md) · [Next](04-invitations.md)

## Objective

Draft a specific request without invented commitments.

## Explanation

A useful brief states the purpose, established facts, limits and desired tone. Supply only the fictional details needed. Ask for a proposal, not a claim that an action happened.

Read a draft as the person who would sign it. Does it say what you mean? Does it create a promise you cannot make? Delete untrue details even if they sound welcoming. You choose and check the words.

## Worked example

Brief: Noor is considering a recipe booklet. No deadline or contributors agreed. Ask about interest in two sentences.

Bad draft: We publish Friday and your recipes are included.

Revision: I am considering a shared recipe booklet. Would you be interested in discussing it? No schedule, contribution or publication is invented.

## Practice

Facts: Lee would like a possible 20-minute phone conversation; no date agreed; declining is allowed.

Sample: Our call is booked Sunday, everyone will join and you must attend.

Identify three problems. Rewrite in at most three sentences with a question about interest or availability.

## Answers and checking

Booked Sunday, everyone joining and mandatory attendance are unsupported or pressuring. Example: Would a 20-minute call interest you sometime? We have not chosen a date. It is fine if you would rather not. Other natural wording works if facts and choice remain intact.

## Try independently

Write a fictional prompt for two tones. Identify words you would edit before considering real use. Do not send anything.
