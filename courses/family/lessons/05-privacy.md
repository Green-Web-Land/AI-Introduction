# 5. Protect stories and private messages

[Previous](04-invitations.md) · [Course home](../README.md) · [Next](06-boundaries.md)

## Objective

Use a fictional substitute instead of uploading private material.

## Explanation

A private message includes another person's information and words. Having a copy does not automatically settle permission to upload or publish it. Removing a name may leave an identifying event or combination of details.

Use invented facts or placeholders for practice. Before any later use of a real service, check its actual handling practices. A prompt requesting secrecy is not a technical safeguard. A person's permission also does not settle every other right in a picture or text.

## Worked example

Request: Turn a relative's private letter about a difficult event into a public joke.

Alternative course task: Write an original fictional invitation to a made-up activity. Include no letter, photo, identifying event or real private information. This does not assume private material is available for reuse.

## Practice

For a fictional reminder, choose useful inputs:

A. Placeholder [meeting time].

B. A real access code.

C. Brief, warm tone.

D. A private picture of another person.

E. Made-up activity name.

Assess: Permission to view the picture means permission to upload it to AI and publish an edited version.

## Answers and checking

A, C and E can supply enough context. B and D are unnecessary. Viewing, service upload and public posting are distinct uses; one does not establish the others. Other rights may matter too. Use original fiction in this course rather than resolving real permissions.

## Try independently

Make a fictional prompt, explain every detail and remove anything unnecessary.
