# Source notes — for adults

Checked 2026-09-11. These references inform limited factual explanations and adult guidance. They do not validate this course, certify age suitability or endorse publication. External pages may discuss online harms in detail: **do not open them as a child activity.**

## S1 — Generated content and privacy

[eSafety Commissioner: parent handout on AI risks, April 2026](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-04/AI-Harms-webinar-summary-term2-2026.pdf).

Used for examples of tools producing words/images, the distinction from human interaction, privacy concerns and help-seeking. The course does not adopt every statement in the handout, comparative claims about children's/adults' skills, harm statistics or product rules. Adult guidance only; Australian service/reporting arrangements are not presented as Canadian requirements.

## S2 — Pattern learning

[IBM: What is machine learning?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/machine-learning).

Used for the limited explanation that many AI systems learn patterns from examples. The shape-row game is an original teaching analogy, not a working AI or an account of all AI. No assertion that every conversation retrains a model.

## S3 — Child-centred principles

[UNICEF: Guidance on AI and children, version 3.0, December 2025](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children).

The report overview identifies privacy, safety, fairness, inclusion, transparency and children's interests as priorities. It informs adult responsibility and editorial boundaries. These are cited guidance principles, not a claim that this course implements legal compliance, technical safeguards or UNICEF certification. The overview was consulted; no exhaustive review of the full policy report is claimed.

## S4 — Outputs can be wrong

[IBM Research: Debugging LLMs to improve their credibility](https://research.ibm.com/blog/debugging-LLMs-for-reliability).

Used only to support checking AI-generated claims rather than assuming fluent output is correct. The three-star/four-answer example is newly authored fiction. It is not a product test, error rate or finding from the cited research.

## S5 — Seek help with uncomfortable content

[eSafety Commissioner: Being safe online](https://www.esafety.gov.au/kids/I-want-help-with/being-safe-online).

Used for the limited practice of telling a trusted adult when an online experience is uncomfortable. The course does not import platform age thresholds, live online tasks or local reporting numbers from this page.

## S6 — Calm adult support

[eSafety Commissioner: The hard-to-have conversations](https://www.esafety.gov.au/parents/issues-and-advice/hard-to-have-conversations).

Used for calm, age-adapted support and the option of another trusted adult. This adult reference includes mature subjects; none of its explicit scenarios is copied into the lessons. A child's actual concern calls for appropriate support, not a classroom investigation.

## Original design decisions and limits

All stories, cards, response examples, diagrams and future scenarios are original AI-assisted educational drafting. Five years is a storytelling horizon, not a forecast. Choice, acknowledgement of help and inclusive participation are educational practices; they are not a comprehensive copyright, school-policy or legal framework.

The course provides no software access, account eligibility, collection form or learner research. No independent child-development review, learner pilot, formal accessibility assessment or exhaustive legal opinion has been obtained. Sources support the mapped claims, not those missing forms of validation.
