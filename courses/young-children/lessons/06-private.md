# 6. Keep private things private

[Course home](../README.md) · [Adult guide](../PARENT-EDUCATOR-GUIDE.md)

**For a grown-up to read with a child.** All stories and answer cards are invented. No AI tool or account is used.

## Our idea

Choose made-up information for a paper story.

## Read together

Some things about people are private. We do not put our address, passwords or personal pictures into a tool for this course.

We use made-up characters instead. If a screen asks for private things, stop and ask a trusted grown-up.

## Little story

A story card needs a setting. Sam chooses “a cloud castle.” Another card asks for a child's real home address. The adult puts that card away without filling it in.

“Our story can happen in an imaginary place,” says the adult.

## Try together

Choose a setting: a cloud castle, a moon garden, or a child's real home address. Only choose the made-up settings. Do not say, write or draw your own address. No real password, photograph or account is part of this activity.

## For the grown-up

Cloud castle and moon garden are the supplied fictional choices. Never ask a child to demonstrate that they know a private detail. The rule is not a technical privacy guarantee: people operating tools must protect information, and even a prompt saying “keep this private” does not implement that protection. Sources S1 and S3. [Source notes for adults](../SOURCES.md).

## Try another way

Make another imaginary place, or point to one supplied choice. If a child starts sharing something personal, gently stop the recording/writing and return to the fictional cards without blame.
