# 4. Tell us what you mean

[Course home](../README.md) · [Adult guide](../PARENT-EDUCATOR-GUIDE.md)

**For a grown-up to read with a child.** All stories and answer cards are invented. No AI tool or account is used.

## Our idea

Add a useful detail to a fictional request.

## Read together

Sometimes “make something” is hard to follow. We can say what we want.

We can practise with a grown-up on paper. We do not need to type into a tool.

## Little story

Ari says, “Give me an idea.” The adult asks, “An idea for what?”

Ari tries: “Suggest a name for a pretend boat. Use one short word.” The adult offers the invented name “Pebble.” Ari may choose another.

## Try together

Try the supplied request: “Suggest a name for a pretend garden. Use one short word.” Choose from Leaf, Garden Full of Dancing Moons, or your own short name. Then change the request to ask for two words.

No names of real children, schools or places are needed.

## For the grown-up

Leaf fits the first stated request. The longer title does not fit its one-word limit. For two words, Moon Garden is one possible answer. This is an original communication exercise, not evidence that a detailed prompt ensures accurate or appropriate AI output. The adult reads and writes as needed. [Source notes for adults](../SOURCES.md).

## Try another way

The adult suggests an answer with the wrong number of words. Say “Let's try again,” or point to the part that needs changing. A child does not have to write or count without support.
