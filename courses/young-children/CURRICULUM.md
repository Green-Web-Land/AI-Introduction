# Curriculum — Young Children

**Adult-led; approximately ages 6–8.** The adult previews each lesson and chooses a suitable pace. There is no required reading level, timetable, quiz score or prerequisite. This is an editorial design, not an evaluated developmental programme.

| Lesson | One learning idea |
|---|---|
| [People and tools](lessons/01-people-tools.md) | Tell the difference between a person and an AI tool. |
| [Ideas a tool can offer](lessons/02-helpful-ideas.md) | Recognise an idea as something people can choose to use. |
| [A little pattern game](lessons/03-patterns.md) | Notice that a pattern can suggest a next item without proving it. |
| [Tell us what you mean](lessons/04-ask-clearly.md) | Add a useful detail to a fictional request. |
| [Look and check](lessons/05-check-answers.md) | Compare an invented answer with supplied evidence. |
| [Keep private things private](lessons/06-private.md) | Choose made-up information for a paper story. |
| [Kind and fair choices](lessons/07-kind-fair.md) | Choose a respectful action instead of obeying an unfair suggestion. |
| [Stop and ask for help](lessons/08-help.md) | Practise a simple help request without seeing upsetting material. |
| [Make it your own](lessons/09-make.md) | Make a choice and describe where an idea came from. |
| [Imagine tomorrow](lessons/10-tomorrow.md) | Distinguish a possible future from a promise. |

## A gentle learning route

Read together, try the supplied card, ask what helped with the choice, then repeat another way if the child wants. The adult handles reading, counting or drawing support. An answer may be a gesture or selection rather than a sentence. Do not treat speed, speech, handwriting or compliance as evidence of understanding.

Lessons 1–3 introduce tools and patterns. Lessons 4–5 practise clear requests and checking. Lessons 6–8 consider privacy, respect and help. Lessons 9–10 return to creativity, choice and uncertain futures.

The final three cards revisit checking, fictional information and seeking help. They do not authorise a child to use an AI service. Use the [adult guide](PARENT-EDUCATOR-GUIDE.md) for suggested observations and alternatives.

## Materials

The lesson text and optional picture aids are enough. Paper and drawing tools may be used; printing, screens, scissors, downloads and live AI are not required. Read picture descriptions if images are unavailable. No real personal data, physical experiment or contact exercise.
