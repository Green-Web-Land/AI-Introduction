# 8. Check practical claims before acting

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Match a claim to the right evidence and recognise limits.

## Explanation

Practical suggestions can have consequences. Do not treat fluent wording as a substitute for relevant instructions or qualified help. This course includes no electrical, gas, chemical, medical or other hazardous procedure.

For product use, find the applicable manufacturer instructions; for specialised concerns, seek an appropriate qualified source. If the needed evidence is unavailable, leave the claim unresolved. Reasking the same tool is not independent verification.

## Worked example

Fictional product card for a paper display stand: “Indoor use only; up to 4 sheets.” A suggestion says, “Use it outdoors with 8 sheets; it works everywhere.”

The suggestion contradicts both supplied limits. Reject it for this exercise. This invented card does not describe a real product or establish real-world safety.

## Practice

A second suggestion uses the stand indoors with 3 sheets. Does it match the supplied limits? Does that establish that an actual stand in your home is safe for that use?

## Answer and reasoning

Attempt the practice before reading this section.

It matches the fictional limits, but says nothing about an actual product, its condition or instructions. Do not transfer an invented specification to real equipment. This exercise tests matching claims, not product certification.

## Transfer task

A tool offers a household repair involving an unfamiliar hazardous system. State a next step without following or reproducing its instructions. A suitable answer is to stop the proposed procedure and obtain relevant qualified guidance. This is a boundary lesson, not an emergency-response or repair manual.
