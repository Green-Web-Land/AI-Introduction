# 3. Describe useful constraints

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Give enough context without supplying private details.

## Explanation

A useful brief names the goal, time, materials and preferences. Missing information should stay missing. Clear instructions help you review a suggestion; they do not guarantee that a model follows them.

Households differ in space, resources, ability and interests. A plan should allow choices and changes rather than assume everyone can stand, travel, buy things or take part in the same way.

## Worked example

Our fictional packet:
- Total time: 60 minutes.
- Quiet indoor activities; paper and pencils only.
- Seated or spoken alternatives welcome.
- No purchases, account, trip or device control.
- Include 10 minutes for a break and 5 for putting materials away.

Brief: “Suggest a paper activity plan within this packet. Show durations and alternatives; mark any missing information.”

## Practice

Identify what is wrong with “Plan the perfect afternoon for everyone.” Rewrite it using the packet, without adding real personal details.

## Answer and reasoning

Attempt the practice before reading this section.

“Perfect” and “everyone” conceal preferences and limits. A better request uses the 60-minute limit, quiet setting and supplied materials, and asks for options. It leaves the learner free to choose or decline rather than promise a universally suitable plan.

## Transfer task

The fictional time limit changes to 40 minutes. Which constraints change automatically? Only the total time. Do not silently remove the break or assume new materials. Ask how to shorten the activities while preserving the other stated limits.
