# 1. Choose help that fits

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Choose a small task and a way to check the result.

## Explanation

AI is optional. For a total, a calculator may be enough; for a reminder, a paper note may do. Generative AI can suggest wording or possibilities, but a reply can be wrong. [Cyber Centre guidance](../SOURCES.md) supports checking output and being careful with inputs.

Start with a goal, available information and what you will decide yourself. Do not give a tool a whole household to manage when you only want a short activity list.

## Worked example

A fictional adult wants ideas for a quiet indoor hour using paper and pencils. A bounded task asks for three options within those materials. A calculator cannot invent story themes, but it can check the time total. Neither tool decides what the person enjoys.

## Practice

For each task, name a reasonable method: add three supplied costs; invent a story opening; remember a chosen time. Explain how you would check it and when you might use no tool.

## Answer and reasoning

Attempt the practice before reading this section.

A calculator or paper arithmetic can add costs; your imagination or optional suggestions can start a story; a note can hold a reminder. Check numbers, choose wording yourself and confirm the time. There is no need to use AI for every step.

## Transfer task

Write a one-sentence brief for a fictional activity plan. Name the output and one limit. Example: “Suggest two quiet paper activities for 30 minutes; do not assume I can buy materials.” Then explain why a suggestion is not a commitment to do it.
