# Parent and teacher guide

[Home](README.md) · [Curriculum](CURRICULUM.md)

## Before you begin

Read the sample yourself. Use paper, spoken answers or large text to suit the learner. These materials target ages 9–12 but do not establish suitability for every child. Offer a choice to skip or pause. No score, AI account or child registration is needed.

Ask “What do you think?” before showing the example answer. Give time to explain. Treat mistakes as clues for the next attempt. A child who spots an error should practise explaining how they know, not simply distrusting everything.

## Run the two samples

1. Read lesson 01 together. Let the child sort the helpers before discussing the suggested answers.
2. Ask the child to explain one AI limit in their own words.
3. In lesson 04, leave the fact card visible. Let the child compare the pretend answer line by line.
4. Ask for a corrected plan and one fact that remains unknown.
5. Finish with a different small task and ask what they would check. Do not equate completion with validated learning effectiveness.

Allow oral responses, drawing, a scribe or extra time. Avoid competition over who is “best at AI.” Different access, language and reading needs do not measure a child's worth or ability.

## Continue through the full edition

After the first two samples, use the curriculum to choose the next lesson. Each offers an objective, invented situation, practice and suggested reasoning. Revisit asking clearly and checking answers whenever another activity needs them. For the future map, accept different interests and avoid treating imagined milestones as deadlines. For fair rules, keep responsibility for systems with adults and providers. Use the glossary and invite the child to explain a word in their own way. No answer needs to be uploaded or scored.

## Optional live demonstration

The offline activities are complete on their own. Before any optional demonstration, an adult must check the chosen service's current age rules, school requirements, privacy controls and suitability. Adult supervision does not override a service's rules. Do not create an underage account, share adult credentials with a child or ask a child to bypass restrictions.

Use only invented prompts and information. Preview outputs before showing them. Do not upload children's names, work, photos, voices, school details or private conversations for this activity. No tool is endorsed by these samples.

## When an answer is upsetting or unsafe

Stop the interaction. Reassure the child that asking for help was the right thing to do. They do not need to keep talking to a chatbot or keep its requests secret. Help them speak to a trusted adult; if the first adult cannot help, try another. Do not require them to copy or reshare upsetting material. Use appropriate adult reporting routes when needed.

Friendly AI wording is not evidence of care, truth or confidentiality. It must not replace trusted human relationships. Do not use these activities to obtain health advice, assess a child's mental health or make consequential decisions about them.

## Feedback and privacy

This repository is educational text, not a child-facing intake service. Do not ask children to open GitHub accounts, post their answers, contact maintainers or submit identifying examples. Adults can discuss wording with the owner using invented examples; keep children's details and work out of repository issues and review records.

The publisher's release process includes review of the candidate's rights, claims, privacy language and child-facing boundaries. That internal AI review is not professional legal certification or proof of educational effectiveness. Any later learner trial needs a separate plan for consent, safeguarding and data handling.
