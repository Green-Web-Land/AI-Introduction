# 08 — Imagine your future

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Word help](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Your mission:** Separate a possible future from a promise.

Use paper, drawing or spoken answers with a trusted adult. No AI account is needed.

## Three possible paths

No one can promise exactly what your world will be like in five years. Tools, schools and your interests may change. You also do not need to decide your future job now.

Imagine you enjoy telling stories. These are invented possibilities, not forecasts:

- **In one year:** regular reading and writing practice could help you explain your ideas more clearly. An allowed AI tool might offer ideas you check.
- **In five years:** you might make a comic with friends, using drawing, writing and perhaps some AI assistance.
- **Another path:** you might discover that you prefer gardening or music. You can learn those skills with people, books and practice, with or without AI.

Access to a tool is not a measure of your talent or worth. Adults, schools and providers are responsible for making safe and fair opportunities; children do not carry that job alone.

## Your turn: draw a future map

Choose an interest. Make four boxes:

1. Something I want to try this month.
2. A skill I might build in one year.
3. A project I could enjoy in five years.
4. A different path if my interests or tools change.

Under your map, add:

- One action I can choose now.
- One thing I cannot control.
- One person or offline resource that could help.
- One way I could notice progress.

Do not put real names, school details or private plans into an online tool. Keep the activity on paper or talk it through.

## Check a worked map

Interest: drawing. Action now: practise three simple shapes each week. One-year possibility: make a short comic. Five-year possibility: join a creative group. Alternative: switch to making models. Uncertainty: what clubs and tools will be available. Progress check: explain one technique and compare two drawings without judging your worth by them.

These are possibilities, not deadlines.

**Finish:** change “AI will make me successful” into a statement you can check. For example: “I will practise explaining one story idea this week and ask for feedback.”

Learning matters even when your future turns out differently. Your job is to grow as a person, not to train AI.

All characters, situations and pretend AI responses in this lesson are invented teaching examples.
