# 04 — Be an answer detective

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [First sample](01-what-is-ai.md)

**Your mission:** check an answer against facts you can see.

You need paper and a pencil, or you can answer aloud. No AI tool is needed.

## The fact card

A fictional craft club has these rules:

- Six children will attend.
- Each child needs two paper stars.
- The club has ten paper stars.
- The activity lasts twenty minutes.
- Use paper and crayons only. Do not use glitter.
- The card says nothing about the weather.

The club asks a pretend AI helper: “Use our fact card to plan the activity. Tell us what is missing.”

## A convincing answer

The following answer is deliberately invented with mistakes:

> Everything is ready! Six children need ten stars, so there are enough. Spend twenty-five minutes decorating with glitter. It will definitely be sunny.

Before reading further, mark each part **matches**, **does not match**, or **we do not know**.

## Work one clue together

“Six children need ten stars.”

Check the card: each of the six children needs two. Draw six pairs or calculate 6 × 2 = 12. The club needs twelve stars. It has ten, so two more are needed.

You found the mistake by checking. You did not need a second chatbot to agree.

## Your turn

1. Check the proposed time.
2. Check the proposed materials.
3. Check the weather claim.
4. Write a corrected answer of three or four sentences.
5. Explain how you checked one sentence.

## Compare your reasoning

- Twenty-five minutes does not fit the twenty-minute limit.
- Glitter breaks the materials rule.
- The weather is unknown from the card. “Definitely sunny” is unsupported; it is not proof that rain is certain either.
- “Everything is ready” overlooks the two missing stars.

One possible correction:

> We need twelve paper stars, so prepare two more. Use paper and crayons only. Decorate for fifteen minutes and tidy for five minutes. The card does not tell us the weather.

The fifteen/five split is a suggested plan, not a fact supplied by the card. Another split totalling twenty can work.

## A new case

A fictional reading group has four children. Each needs one bookmark. There are three bookmarks. Someone says, “We have enough.”

What would you check? How many are missing?

Suggested answer: four bookmarks are needed; one is missing. Explain with a drawing or subtraction.

## Your detective habit

**Read the task → find the facts → compare → fix → explain.**

For a real-world claim, the fact card may not be enough: ask a trusted adult to help find an appropriate source. Another AI repeating an answer does not make it true.

Never use a confident AI answer alone to decide whether something dangerous is safe. Stop and ask a trusted adult.

Every club, fact card and pretend output in this lesson is fictional. You do not need to send us your answers.
