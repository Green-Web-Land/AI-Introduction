# 01 — What is AI?

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Next sample](04-answer-detective.md)

**Your mission:** explain one thing AI can help with and one reason to check it.

You need paper and a pencil, or someone to talk with. No computer is needed.

## Meet three helpers

Mina is making a pretend space-garden story. She has three computer helpers:

- A calculator adds six seeds and four seeds.
- A timer rings after ten minutes.
- An AI writing tool suggests a name for a garden on the Moon.

All three use software. Doing a useful job does not automatically make a tool AI.

AI is a name for computer systems built to do tasks such as recognising patterns, making predictions or generating words and pictures. Many AI systems learn patterns from examples during development. They can use those patterns to make an answer for a new request.

An AI writing tool can offer “Moon Sprout Garden.” Mina can use it, change it or choose her own name. The idea is a suggestion. Mina remains the author making the choice.

## Useful does not mean always right

Imagine the tool also says: “Plants on the Moon never need water.”

That sounds like a fact, but sounding sure is not proof. Mina should check a suitable science source with an adult before using it as a fact.

A tool might write “I'm happy to help!” Friendly words do not make it a person who knows you or a trusted adult. It can make mistakes and may not have the information your question needs.

An AI answer also does not automatically do a real-world job. A suggested shopping list does not buy anything. Some applications connect AI to tools that can act; adults need to understand and control those permissions.

## Your turn: helper cards

For each card, choose **ordinary fixed task**, **AI example**, or **not enough information**.

1. A timer follows the setting “ring in ten minutes.”
2. A tool trained on examples suggests a new ending for a story.
3. An app says “I am your clever helper,” but tells you nothing about how it works.

Now finish these sentences in your own words:

- AI could help me think of…
- Before I trust a factual answer, I can…
- I can decide not to use a suggestion because…

## Check together

1. Ordinary fixed task: this timer example does not need AI.
2. AI example: it generates an ending using learned patterns.
3. Not enough information: an advertising phrase does not explain the technology.

Possible answers: “two story titles”; “compare it with a reliable book and ask an adult”; “it does not fit my idea.” Other reasoned answers can work.

**Takeaway:** AI can help make suggestions. You still choose, think and check.

All characters and answers above are invented teaching examples, not a record of a real AI test.
