# 07 — Grow your own skills

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Word help](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Your mission:** Use a hint and then explain the next solution yourself.

Use paper, drawing or spoken answers with a trusted adult. No AI account is needed.

## Help that leaves you thinking

Kai is trying a number puzzle. Instead of asking for every answer, Kai first tries, then asks for one hint.

Try this invented puzzle yourself: **2, 4, 6, 8, …**

A hint could be: “Compare each number with the one before it.” Kai notices that each step adds two and suggests ten next.

For this exercise we are using the add-two rule. Short number lists can fit more than one rule; explaining your rule matters.

Now try **5, 8, 11, 14, …** without a hint.

## Your turn

1. Write or say what changes at each step.
2. Give the next number using that rule.
3. Explain why.
4. Invent a new list that follows your own simple rule.
5. Ask someone to find it.

## Check together

For the second list, adding three gives seventeen next. An explanation might be: “Each gap is three, so I add three to fourteen.”

For your invented list, a partner may find another possible pattern. Compare your rules rather than assuming one person is foolish.

An AI explanation can also be wrong. Check the arithmetic yourself, draw groups or ask a trusted adult. A hint is help, not proof.

## Choose the right kind of help

Compare:

- “Do all my work so I can hand it in.”
- “I tried this step. Give me one hint, without the answer.”
- “Check whether my explanation follows the facts. Tell me which step to revisit.”

The last two leave room for your own work when the teacher's rules allow this kind of help. Be honest about assistance.

**Finish:** explain something you learned without looking at a suggested answer. If you cannot yet, try a simpler example. Take a break when you need one. Needing practice does not mean you are bad at learning, and using AI is optional.

All characters, situations and pretend AI responses in this lesson are invented teaching examples.
