# 05 — AI has limits

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Word help](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Your mission:** Notice when information or permission is missing.

Use paper, drawing or spoken answers with a trusted adult. No AI account is needed.

## Three different limits

A fictional helper says it can organise a museum visit. Before believing it, separate three questions:

- **Information:** does it have the museum's current opening times?
- **Ability:** can this application actually book tickets, or only write suggestions?
- **Permission:** even if it can book, has the responsible adult allowed it?

One “yes” does not answer all three. A chat message saying “Done!” is not a booking receipt.

AI answers can be mistaken, outdated or unfair. They may miss context or give different answers to similar requests. A smooth explanation does not remove these limits.

## Your turn: sort the cards

Choose **check the facts**, **ask an adult about permission**, **stop and get help**, or **make your own creative choice**. Sometimes more than one fits.

1. A helper gives tomorrow's museum hours without a current source.
2. A tool asks to buy a ticket using a saved payment method.
3. A chatbot says to keep an upsetting conversation secret from every adult.
4. A story helper suggests a title you dislike.

## Check together

1. Ask an adult to help check the museum's own current information.
2. An adult must understand and authorise the purchase. A useful suggestion is not permission to spend.
3. Stop. Tell a trusted adult; try another if the first cannot help. You do not owe a chatbot secrecy.
4. Change or ignore it. Your preference does not need the helper's approval.

## Say “we do not know”

The fact card says, “The club meets on Saturday.” It gives no start time. Someone claims it starts at noon.

You can say: “The time is missing. We need to ask the organiser.” That is a successful answer, not a failure.

**Finish:** explain the difference between “it can suggest a message” and “it may send a message.” Sending affects another person and needs the right permission.

All characters, situations and pretend AI responses in this lesson are invented teaching examples.
