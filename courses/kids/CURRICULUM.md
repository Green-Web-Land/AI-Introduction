# Your AI adventure

[Home](README.md)

For ages 9–12. Try one activity at a time with a trusted adult. You can answer aloud, draw or write. No live AI tool is required.

| Lesson | What you will practise | Activity | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 — What is AI? | Explain one useful ability and one limit | Sort three computer helpers | [Read lesson](lessons/01-what-is-ai.md) |
| 02 — Ideas you can use | Choose a small task while keeping your own choices | Invent two endings to a fictional story | [Read lesson](lessons/02-useful-ideas.md) |
| 03 — Ask clearly | Give a task, facts and limits | Ask for a pretend picnic plan | [Read lesson](lessons/03-ask-clearly.md) |
| 04 — Be an answer detective | Check an answer against given facts | Find mistakes in a club plan | [Read lesson](lessons/04-answer-detective.md) |
| 05 — AI has limits | Notice when information or permission is missing | Sort “check”, “ask an adult” and “stop” cards | [Read lesson](lessons/05-ai-has-limits.md) |
| 06 — Never Do with AI | Protect privacy and avoid harm | Replace unsafe choices with safer ones | [Read lesson](lessons/06-never-do.md) |
| 07 — Grow your own skills | Try first and explain what you learned | Use a hint, then solve a new puzzle yourself | [Read lesson](lessons/07-grow-your-skills.md) |
| 08 — Imagine your future | Separate possibilities from promises | Draw a one-year and five-year learning path | [Read lesson](lessons/08-imagine-your-future.md) |
| 09 — Fair rules for AI | Connect fairness, choice and responsibility to actions | Design rules for a fictional classroom helper | [Read lesson](lessons/09-fair-rules.md) |

Lesson 06 covers private information and pictures, hurtful impersonation, made-up evidence, unchecked important advice and actions a child does not understand. It teaches children to seek help without blame.

Lesson 08 uses “could” and “might,” shows more than one possible future, and includes a future without AI. No career, intelligence or income promises. Children choose a skill to practise now; they do not need to predict their adult life.

Lesson 09 distinguishes our proposed principles from laws, a service's rules and protections built into software. Children deserve explanations, fair treatment and a way to question decisions. An instruction to “be safe” does not make a tool safe.

## Review goal

The first edition aims to let a learner explain a useful AI ability, name a limit, check supplied facts and ask for help. These are proposed learning outcomes, not measured results. Allow roughly 10–15 minutes per sample, adjust freely, and take breaks; times are editorial estimates.

All nine lessons are included in this first edition. Read the parent/teacher guide before activities. A glossary and reuse licence accompany this edition.
