# Learning together and reviewing the course

[Course home](README.md)

This guide supports discussion and outlines a possible formative learner review. It does not report a completed trial.

## A welcoming session

Invite learners to connect a lesson with something they care about. Use the supplied fictional situations when someone prefers not to discuss their life or work. Spoken responses, reading support, calculators and accessibility aids are welcome. Ask about useful accommodations without collecting unnecessary personal details.

For each lesson: read the opening situation, let people attempt a response, compare the reasoning and then try the changed example. Ask “What supports your choice?” and “What would change your mind?” A learner may reasonably choose a non-AI method or disagree with a suggested social arrangement if they explain the reasons.

Use breaks and a pace that fits the group. The lesson times are estimates. Do not grade reading speed, digital confidence, accent or agreement with the facilitator. Let people pass on sharing an answer.

## What to look for

Use “explained independently,” “explained with support” or “revisit.” Record what the material needs to explain better rather than ranking people.

| Lesson | Evidence of understanding | Misunderstanding to explore |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Everyday life | Finds two contradictions and an unsupported claim; explains a choice | Treating fluent wording as evidence |
| 2. Working with AI | Preserves the task requirements; totals 20 and then 12 minutes | Revising time but overlooking changed materials |
| 3. Evidence and trust | Traces repeated claims to one source; updates approval status only | Treating repetition as independent evidence or approval as success |
| 4. Changing work | Gets 18 and 23 minutes; names a skill and an organisational question | Equating a task with an entire job or counting drafting time only |
| 5. AI in society | Distinguishes 4× messages from 2× participants; notices missing residents and values | Equating app responses or attendance with the whole purpose of a service |
| 6. Skills | Explains 30 ÷ 3 = 10 and 36 ÷ 4 = 9; adapts a creative choice to its purpose | Copying an answer without understanding which items or criteria count |
| 7. Responsibility | Retains all suggestions; separates grouping from ranking and sending | Treating information permission or a nominal reviewer as sufficient for any use |
| 8. Next 30 days | Makes a checkable plan; notices the changed finish time; explains a bounded final-case proposal | Treating a timetable or a successful small task as a guarantee |

These are editorial learning checks, not a qualification. Independent explanation can be spoken or written and can use the person's normal accessibility aids.

## A possible formative review

For a small formative review, agree the purpose and arrangements, then invite 8–12 willing adults with varied digital confidence and access needs. This is a practical editorial sample, not a representative study. Agree the purpose and feedback handling; people may pause or decline an activity. Keep identifying details and learner records out of GitHub.

Observe whether readers can find the first lesson, understand the stories, attempt the tasks and use an idea in a different situation. Ask what was useful, confusing, patronising or missing. Separate observed explanations from enjoyment or stated confidence.

The eight-lesson route can be split over several sessions. A brief first session can focus on navigation and one lesson; it does not validate the whole course. Include work/society lessons and the final case in later review rather than judging the course only by factual error detection.

## Revision decisions

Correct wrong answers, unclear task facts or inaccessible essentials before publication. Repeated misunderstandings may indicate missing explanations. Add those explanations and review them again. Useful feedback can also suggest a more relevant example or a shorter route.

A small trial can reveal problems; it cannot establish employment outcomes, long-term learning benefits or suitability for every adult. When sharing findings, describe what participants tried, what you observed and the limits of those observations.

Edition 0.2.1 is published for learning and feedback. Learner effectiveness has not yet been tested; keep that limitation clear when describing the course.
