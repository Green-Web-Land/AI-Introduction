# Lesson and visual template

For editors and educators. [Course home](README.md)

## The central message

Use exactly: **Your choices matter. Learn. Question. Decide.**

Give it three jobs: a prominent welcome, a repeatable lesson structure and a closing reflection. The phrase should describe what the learner does. Avoid treating personal choice as control over every outcome; identify the responsibilities of institutions and other decision-makers where relevant.

## Lesson structure

1. **Title and outcome:** a familiar situation and something the learner can demonstrate.
2. **Opening story:** a person with a recognisable need, enough supplied facts and a meaningful choice.
3. **Learn:** explain one main idea in plain language, connected to the story.
4. **Question:** inspect evidence, missing information and effects on other people.
5. **Decide:** show a reasoned choice, the next action and a reason to revisit it.
6. **Your turn:** provide all required materials and invite a response before revealing guidance.
7. **Compare your reasoning:** explain why an answer works and recognise reasonable alternatives.
8. **Try a different situation:** change a meaningful condition or the kind of task.
9. **Take it with you:** a portable question or small next step.

Keep instructions concrete. Put a source beside the factual claim it supports. Clearly label invented records once when introducing them. Longer provenance, publication and testing notes belong in supporting pages.

## Visual system

- Forest green supplies the primary surface; warm paper and white reading surfaces make long text calm. A restrained amber accent marks the act of choosing.
- Use a large, readable display treatment for “Your choices matter.” Keep the course name visible beside it.
- Present Learn, Question and Decide as three labelled steps. On narrow screens they stack in reading order; meaning never depends on colour or arrows alone.
- Main lesson body text is at least 17 CSS pixels in the screen preview, with comfortable line spacing and a reading column around 68 characters. Navigation, tables and supporting labels have separate compact styles.
- Use story extracts, fact cards and purposeful diagrams. Keep instructional text as live text.
- Each course entry shows its purpose, an estimated effort and an outcome.
- Reveal answer guidance with native disclosure controls. Print includes the answers.
- Provide clear focus states, underlined reading links, labelled navigation and usable touch targets.
- Default to no animation, account, analytics, external font or required network asset.

## Variants with a purpose

The cover uses larger display type. Lesson pages use a narrow reading column. Worksheets use wider tables and writing space. The print version adds page breaks between lessons and workbook sections and includes all answer guidance. These are intentional education layouts; they do not alter the WebAI UI standard or application.

## Content and preview relationship

Markdown is the authoritative lesson source. The local preview and printable review copy are generated from it. Correct the source and regenerate both outputs to avoid maintaining separate versions of the teaching text.

Before publishing a new edition, check learning outcomes, source support, arithmetic, local links, keyboard use, narrow layouts and the printed copy. Describe learner testing separately from these author checks.
