# Words used in this course

[Course home](README.md)

These are plain-language teaching explanations. Return to a lesson for an example; you do not need to memorise the list.

| Word | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| AI | Computer systems that use inputs to produce outputs such as predictions, recommendations or generated content |
| Generative AI | AI that can produce content such as words or pictures |
| Prompt | An instruction or question given to an AI system |
| Task brief | The purpose, supplied information, boundaries, desired result and checks for a task |
| Evidence | Information used to support or challenge a particular claim |
| Source | Where information came from; examine its context and support as well as its identity |
| Independent confirmation | Support that does not merely repeat the same original claim |
| Uncertainty | Something not established or not known with enough confidence for the decision |
| Scenario | A possible situation used to explore choices; it is not a prediction that it will happen |
| Task exposure | The possibility that a task could be affected by a technology's capabilities; not a prediction of job loss |
| Representation | Whose experiences or responses appear in information, and whose are missing |
| Trade-off | A choice where improving one thing may involve giving up or changing another |
| Mean or average | A total divided by the number of items included; always explain which items count |
| Accountability | A practical arrangement for explaining decisions, taking responsibility and addressing problems |
| Human review | A person examines the work with the knowledge, time and authority needed to question or change it |
| Transfer task | A changed example that checks whether you can use an idea beyond the sample |

[Sources and editorial notes](SOURCES.md) explain the research foundations and the limits of these simplified definitions.
