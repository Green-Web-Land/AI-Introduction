# 6. Draft communication without making promises

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Write a clear draft that remains within the facts.

## Explanation

Choose the intended recipient and purpose before writing. A polite tone does not justify inventing a date, refund, approval or completed action. Distinguish a request for information from a commitment to deliver it.

Use only the fictional packet. The message remains on paper; no sending is part of this lesson. In a real workplace, follow the responsible person's review and communication rules.

## Worked example

Flawed draft: “Your item M will definitely arrive Friday. We have approved compensation.”

The packet supports neither statement. Better internal draft: “Item M is delayed. A revised date has been requested but is not confirmed. Please confirm who will provide the next update.”

This asks a question without assigning an unapproved deadline or promising compensation.

## Practice

Draft a short external-facing message about M for review. Do not claim that a follow-up has happened unless the packet says it. Add an internal note identifying the missing information and approval needed before sending.

## Answer and reasoning

Try the practice before reading this section.

Possible draft: “The revised date for item M is not yet confirmed.” Internal note: “Verify the current status and intended recipient; obtain the required sending approval; no compensation decision is supplied.” The brief draft may need more facts before it becomes useful externally. Mark that need rather than inventing reassurance.

## Transfer task

A colleague wants the phrase “We guarantee this will not happen again.” Explain why the packet does not support it and suggest a limited alternative. Example: “We are reviewing the delay” is also unsupported unless a review is actually underway. A safer next step is to ask for confirmed remediation information before adding such a statement.
