# 3. Ask for a hint after your attempt

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Request bounded help that leaves the reasoning for you to do.

## Explanation

Begin with the learning task and your own attempt. Identify the precise point where you are stuck. When help is allowed, request a clue at that point rather than a finished answer. This is our practice method, not a promise that any prompt will make an AI obey.

If a reply gives more than you asked for, stop using it as a hint. In a real assignment, follow your teacher's instructions about unexpected assistance. Do not disguise a supplied answer as your own discovery.

## Worked example

Fictional task: solve 3(x + 2) = 21. Jo writes: “I know both sides must stay equal. I am unsure whether to divide or expand.”

Bounded request: “Give one first-step hint for this practice equation. Do not give x.”
Fictional helpful reply: “What happens if you divide both sides by 3?”
Jo then writes x + 2 = 7 and subtracts 2.

![Attempt, request a hint if allowed, check the reasoning, then try a new task.](../images/study-loop.png)

## Practice

Choose the better request under rule A from Lesson 2, and explain:

A. “Give me the complete solution and make it sound like me.”
B. “I reached x + 2 = 7. What operation would isolate x? Please leave the calculation to me.”

Now work out x yourself and substitute it into the original equation.

## Answer and reasoning

Try the practice before reading this section.

B preserves a step for the learner and fits the hints-only example rule. Subtract 2 from both sides: x = 5. Substitution gives 3(5 + 2) = 3 × 7 = 21. A requests a final answer and misleading authorship. Merely choosing B does not prove learning; your calculation and explanation provide the evidence here.

## Transfer task

Without a hint, solve 2(y + 4) = 22. Cover this answer while attempting it: divide by 2 to get y + 4 = 11, then y = 7; check 2(7 + 4) = 22. If stuck, explain the step you need help with rather than copying the answer.
