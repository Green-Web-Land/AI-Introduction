# 8. Protect privacy and collaborate respectfully

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Choose safe fictional inputs and keep people in control of their contributions.

## Explanation

For these exercises, use invented material only. Do not enter names, school identifiers, grades, private messages, passwords, photographs or another person's work into an AI service. Removing a name may still leave identifying details. A request in a prompt to “keep this private” is not a technical privacy safeguard.

Our course principles are to preserve choice, privacy, fairness and responsibility. They are distinct from enforceable laws, a school's rules, service terms and actual safeguards. [UNICEF's guidance](../SOURCES.md) informs the child-centred concerns; it is not a worldwide permission slip.

Never use AI to impersonate a classmate, fabricate evidence, bully someone or create humiliating images. If something goes wrong, stop sharing it and seek help from a trusted adult or the school's appropriate contact. Do not circulate harmful content as a demonstration.

## Worked example

A group wants feedback on an argument. They have real classmates' private messages, and a wholly invented three-sentence example. The exercise can use the invented example instead. Each group member can explain their own contribution; no one must disclose personal experiences to participate.

## Practice

Choose a response for each fictional situation:

A. A partner wants to upload the class grade spreadsheet.
B. A partner says removing names makes a private conversation safe to upload.
C. A classmate declines AI use in a shared practice activity.
D. A generated image humiliates someone; a group wants to forward it to “warn everyone.”

## Answer and reasoning

Try the practice before reading this section.

A: do not upload it; use a fictional task. B: names are not the only identifiers, and the conversation is still private; do not use it here. C: respect the refusal and use the paper alternative, with teacher guidance where needed. D: do not amplify it; ask a trusted adult or appropriate school contact how to report the concern safely.

## Transfer task

Invent a harmless two-sentence example about arranging fictional library tables. Check that it includes no real person, private event or disguised classmate. Explain why this substitution is better for our task than trying to sanitise real private messages. This is a course boundary, not a claim that all real data processing is forbidden by law.
