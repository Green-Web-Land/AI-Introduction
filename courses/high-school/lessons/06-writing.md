# 6. Write with your own reasoning

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Use feedback to revise a supported argument while keeping authorship honest.

## Explanation

Start with your claim and evidence. If critique is allowed, ask what is unclear or unsupported; decide which suggestions to accept and why. A more impressive sentence can be less accurate. Preserve the scope of the evidence even when revising for style.

Writing with your own reasoning means you can explain your choices. It is not a technique for disguising generated text or evading school rules. Keep the notes or assistance record your teacher actually requires.

## Worked example

Using the original fictional packet in [Lesson 5](05-sources.md), a first draft says: “The new tables are better because 60% learned more.”

Fictional feedback: “What did the pilot measure?”
Revision: “Twelve of twenty volunteers preferred the new arrangement. This suggests it was popular with some participants, but the pilot did not measure learning.”

The writer changed the claim, not merely the wording. The packet supports a limited preference statement.

## Practice

Build a four-part outline from the original packet:

1. A question the pilot can help address.
2. The relevant evidence.
3. A limitation.
4. A reasonable next question.

Then write a two-sentence paragraph. Reject any suggestion to describe the pilot as proof of improved grades.

## Answer and reasoning

Try the practice before reading this section.

One outline: Did participating volunteers prefer the arrangement? Twelve of twenty did. Volunteers are a limited group and learning was not measured. Would a broader, appropriately designed comparison find similar preferences?

A suitable paragraph reports the count and limitation. It need not match our wording. “The tables raise grades” remains unsupported even if you add a citation to A.

## Transfer task

Using the corrected count of 10 of 20 from Lesson 5's transfer task, revise your paragraph. Answer guidance: change the count to 10 and percentage to 50% if used; identify the correction, retain the limitation, and reconsider words such as “most.” Exactly half is not a majority. Explain one wording choice you made yourself.
