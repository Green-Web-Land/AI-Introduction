# What is AI?

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Your goal:** distinguish the model, the application and its connected tools.

AI is a broad field. A useful starting description is a computer-based system that infers outputs from inputs for a purpose. Outputs can include predictions, generated content or recommendations. Systems differ in how independently they operate. This is a simplified introduction, not a test for deciding whether every product legally counts as AI. [OECD explanatory memorandum](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/explanatory-memorandum-on-the-updated-oecd-definition-of-an-ai-system_623da898-en.html)

This course mainly concerns generative AI: systems used to produce content such as text. AI also includes systems used for classification and prediction. Not every automated task needs AI; a fixed formula can calculate a total without generating an explanation.

## Three layers to recognise

| Layer | Plain meaning | Fictional example |
|---|---|---|
| Model | The computational component used to infer an output. | Produces a proposed paragraph from your request. |
| Application | The software around the model, including interface and workflow. | Shows the paragraph and lets you edit it. |
| Connected tool | A capability the application may be allowed to invoke. | Saves a document or sends a message. |

These layers may be packaged together, but they answer different questions. A good paragraph does not prove the application saved it. A claim that a message was sent does not prove it reached someone. Look for the relevant application's actual evidence.

## A conversation is not the whole system

You provide instructions and information. The service may also use other available context, depending on its design and settings. Do not assume every service has browsing, remembers earlier conversations or can access your files. Check the particular product before relying on such features.

Generating an answer from a conversation is different from training a model. A correction may help shape a later response in that conversation; it does not establish permanent learning. Whether data is retained or used for training is a separate service-specific question.

## Worked example

You ask a fictional assistant, “Draft a reminder using this event description.” It displays a draft. That is content generation. You click Save and see the document in your chosen folder. That is a separate application action. Sending the reminder would be another action requiring the right permission and evidence.

If you only wanted a draft, say so and use a workflow whose actual permissions fit that boundary. Clear words communicate intent; limited access helps enforce it.

## Exercise and answer guidance

Classify these observations: (A) a suggested invitation appears; (B) an email integration reports a successful send; (C) an editor shows a preview; (D) someone replies that they received the invitation.

**Answers:** A concerns generated output; B concerns a connected tool's reported action; C concerns the application interface; D is recipient-side evidence. None alone proves all the others occurred. The examples are authored teaching illustrations, not test results.

You pass when you can explain why “the AI wrote it” and “the system sent it” describe different events. For your next tool, identify what it can produce, what it can access and what it can actually change.
