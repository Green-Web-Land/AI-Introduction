# Learn and grow with AI

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

**Your goal:** design a practice session that ends with something you can explain or do yourself.

Use AI as one possible source of explanations, questions and feedback. Your learning goal comes first. The routine below is an editorial practice suggestion, not a scientifically validated intervention or guarantee of faster learning.

## A five-step practice routine

1. **Choose:** name one small skill and an observable outcome.
2. **Attempt:** try it yourself and record the starting point.
3. **Ask:** request a hint or focused feedback rather than a finished answer immediately.
4. **Check and revise:** compare guidance with a trusted reference or knowledgeable person and correct your work.
5. **Explain unaided:** try a similar example without AI and explain your choices.

If you cannot evaluate the explanation, narrow the topic or involve a suitable teacher or reference. Keep a non-AI route available: a textbook, course, colleague or practice group can remain useful whether or not a service is accessible.

## Worked example: clear instructions

A fictional learner wants to write a short guide for watering a particular plant. Their first attempt says, “Water it properly.” They ask for questions that would make the instruction clearer, not for invented care advice.

Possible authored questions are: “Which plant? What conditions? What does your reliable care reference recommend?” The learner obtains the facts, writes a clearer instruction and asks another beginner which step remains ambiguous. AI has helped structure a question; it has not established the horticultural facts.

The learner later writes instructions for a different everyday task without AI. Can another person follow them? That is more informative about their skill than counting generated pages.

## Keep a small learning record

| Record | Example |
|---|---|
| Skill and baseline | My instructions omit required materials. |
| Practice | Write one short procedure and request questions about missing details. |
| Evidence checked | Compare with the actual task and a reliable reference. |
| Independent result | Explain the procedure without reopening the generated answer. |
| Next adjustment | Practise ordering the steps more clearly. |

Avoid recording private information simply to fill the table. Total effort, mistakes found and independent performance are useful observations; they are not a competition with other learners.

## Exercise

Choose a small skill, such as writing clear meeting notes or explaining a familiar process. Write a practice plan with a starting attempt, a request for feedback, a verification source, an unaided check and a fallback if AI is unavailable.

**Answer guidance:** “I will summarise fictional meeting notes myself, ask which supplied decisions I omitted, compare the feedback with the notes, and summarise a second set without AI. A peer can give feedback if the tool is unavailable.” This is sufficient because progress is observable and the reference is available.

You pass when the exercise develops something you can demonstrate, not merely something the system can produce. Stop or change methods if the session is confusing, unreliable or not helping your goal. Rest and human support belong in learning too.
