# Give clear instructions

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

Lesson AIE-03 · Draft sample · 2026-09-11

**Your goal:** turn a vague request into instructions whose result you can check.

You do not need an AI account for this lesson. Read the examples and complete the exercise on paper or in a text editor.

## Start with the result you need

Imagine asking someone, “Write an invitation.” They would need to know who is invited, what the event is and which details to include. An AI application also needs relevant context in the request or another available source. Do not assume it knows details you have not provided.

A useful request answers five questions:

1. **Goal:** What should the finished work help you do?
2. **Context:** Who is it for, and what facts should it use?
3. **Limits:** What must it avoid or leave unchanged?
4. **Format:** What should the answer look like?
5. **Check:** How will you decide whether it meets your needs?

This is a writing aid, not a magic formula. Clear instructions do not guarantee a correct answer. Generative AI can produce plausible statements that are false; important details still need checking. [NIST's Generative AI Profile, section 2.2](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)

## Worked example

Vague request:

> Write a good invitation for our workshop.

More useful request:

> Draft a friendly invitation for adults attending a beginner gardening workshop. Use only these fictional event facts: title “Grow Together”; date 18 May; time 2–3 p.m.; location Maple Community Room; attendance is free; bring a notebook. Use a subject line and a body of no more than 80 words. Do not invent a year, weekday, address, registration link or organiser contact. If an essential detail is missing, list it after the draft as a question. Draft only; do not send anything.

Here is a possible answer written for this lesson, **not a recorded AI output**:

> **Subject: Join us for Grow Together**
>
> Curious about gardening? Join our beginner workshop at Maple Community Room on 18 May, from 2–3 p.m. Attendance is free. Bring a notebook and come ready to learn.
>
> **Questions before sending:** Which year is the event? Do guests need an address or registration instructions?

Compare the draft with the supplied facts. It preserves the title, date, time, venue, cost and notebook instruction. It stays within the requested length. Missing information appears as questions rather than invented facts. A real invitation still needs the organiser's review before sending.

## Your turn

Use these fictional facts:

- Event: Repair and Reuse.
- Audience: adults new to repairing clothes.
- Date and time: 7 June, 10–11 a.m.; year not supplied.
- Place: Cedar Workshop Room.
- Cost: free.
- Bring: one item of clothing needing a small repair.
- No registration details or contact person have been supplied.

Write a request for a subject line and an invitation body of no more than 70 words. Ask for a welcoming tone, prohibit invented details and keep sending outside the task.

Before reading the answer guidance, underline the goal, context, limits, format and check in your request.

## Check your work

Your request succeeds if another reader can answer all of these:

- What should be produced, and for whom?
- Which event facts must be preserved?
- What information must not be invented?
- What length and structure are required?
- Does the task end at a draft?

One acceptable request is:

> Draft a welcoming invitation for adults new to repairing clothes. Use only the fictional facts below: Repair and Reuse; 7 June, 10–11 a.m.; Cedar Workshop Room; free; bring one item of clothing needing a small repair. Include a subject line and a body of no more than 70 words. Do not invent a year, weekday, address, contact, equipment or registration details. Put questions about missing information after the draft. Draft only; do not send. Check that all supplied event details are included.

There are many valid versions. The checklist matters more than copying this wording. If you try the request with an AI application, compare its answer with the facts yourself. Its own statement that it checked everything is not proof.

## If the result is wrong

Point to the specific mismatch: “You added a registration link that I did not supply. Remove it and list registration as a question.” Then check the revised answer again, including details that were previously correct.

Instructions such as “draft only” express your intent. For applications connected to email or other tools, also check their actual permissions and approval controls before use. A sentence in a prompt is not a technical access restriction.

## Take it with you

For your next small task, write down the desired result and how you will check it before asking AI. Give only the information needed, and use fictional or non-sensitive details when practising.
