# What can AI help with?

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

**Your goal:** choose a small task with a result you can check.

Start with a need, not a feature list. “I need to explain these instructions clearly” is easier to evaluate than “I should use more AI.” The suggestions below are candidate uses to assess with your chosen tool, not promises that every model or application performs them well.

| Task to explore | Useful input | What you must check |
|---|---|---|
| Drafting | Audience, purpose and approved facts | Meaning, accuracy, tone and missing details |
| Organising notes | Non-sensitive notes and categories | Omissions, invented connections and misplaced items |
| Learning | A topic, your attempted answer and a reference | Whether explanations agree with the reference and you can apply them |
| Comparing options | Options and your decision criteria | Unsupported claims and overlooked trade-offs |
| Technical assistance | A small synthetic example and expected behaviour | Correctness and effects in an authorised test environment |

## A useful task has an ending

Define the deliverable and your acceptance check. “Suggest three headings for these notes” has an observable ending. “Manage my business” leaves too many decisions and permissions undefined.

Count preparation, review and correction time. If a calculator or a direct edit solves the task reliably, use it. AI is optional. A task is a poor starting exercise if you cannot recognise a plausible but wrong result.

## Worked example: a volunteer guide

Fictional supplied notes: the room opens at 9; chairs must stay clear of the exit; volunteers sign in at the desk.

Request: “Turn these three facts into a short checklist. Do not add rules. Draft only.”

An acceptable authored illustration is: “Open the room at 9. Keep chairs clear of the exit. Sign in at the desk.” Check that each supplied fact survives. The assistant should not invent a door code, change the opening time or describe the checklist as an official safety inspection.

You could do this manually. The exercise teaches selection and checking, not proof that AI is faster.

## Exercise

Choose an approach for each fictional task and explain why:

1. Add 14 and 27.
2. Suggest three titles for your own short gardening article.
3. Decide whether an unfamiliar contract is safe to sign using only an AI summary.
4. Sort six non-sensitive notes into categories you provide.

**Answer guidance:** use a calculator or mental arithmetic for 1; checked AI suggestions or your own brainstorming are reasonable for 2; do not rely on the summary alone for 3—important missing context and specialist judgment may matter; 4 is a bounded candidate if you compare every item with the originals. Multiple choices are acceptable when justified.

You pass if you name the task, what information is appropriate to use and how you will check the result. Before trying it, also identify one reason to stop, such as unexpected access requests or an output you cannot evaluate.
