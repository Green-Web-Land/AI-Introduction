# Educator and supporter guide

[Course home](README.md)

## Preparation

Read the complete lessons and answer reasoning first. The course is intended for postsecondary learners, primarily adult beginners; suitability has not been tested. Offer spoken, typed or handwritten responses, breaks and explanations of notation as appropriate. Do not infer misconduct or a disability from a learner's response.

No account, live tool, personal disclosure or public submission is required. State the actual rules for any real assignment separately. Our invented cards are practice material, not institutional policy. Some learners may be minors; do not assume age or provider eligibility from enrolment.

## Working with the packet

Lesson 4 supplies all records needed for the core exercises:
A version 1: Q [8,9,10,10,11,12], R [10,11,12,12,13,14].
A version 2 replaces Q's final 12 with 18.
B repeats A; C concerns a different task and group with missing counts/methods; D is an unsupported assistant claim.

Keep labels visible. No record is a real study, publication or participant dataset. A's volunteers chose rooms, and learning retention was not measured. C's equal recorded means do not establish universal equivalence or contradict every possible effect. Students should identify these limits without inventing missing details.

Invite learners to explain the first incorrect inference. Model one step, then let them try the next. A short accurate synthesis is preferable to a confident unsupported conclusion. Do not reward removing relevant uncertainty for style.

## Portfolio feedback

| Part | Evidence to look for | Follow-up if needed |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Names an answerable question and relevant outcome | Separate time, comprehension and long-term learning |
| Permission | Uses the actual fictional card and asks when unclear | Compare requested use with stated permission |
| Citation | Identifies a supplied record and checks the passage | Separate “record found” from “claim supported” |
| Comparison | Distinguishes methods, outcomes and repeated data | Build the small comparison table |
| Analysis/method | Correct version, units, sums, denominator and missing-data rule | Trace inputs one by one |
| Assistance | Describes actual work and help accurately | Compare the statement against the work record |

Use “explained,” “partly explained” or “revisit” as optional feedback about the work, not labels for a person. No validated scoring instrument, AI detector or credential is provided.

## Supporting transfer

Ask how a discipline would define its evidence and method. The course's tiny numerical examples do not replace disciplinary research training. No learner should recruit participants, upload restricted material or run unknown code to finish a lesson. For real work, refer unresolved permissions and methods to the appropriate instructor, supervisor, library or institutional service.

Discuss the future scenarios as possibilities shaped by teaching, access and safeguards. Do not imply that effort or AI access guarantees the same outcomes for everyone.

## Reuse

Keep fictional labels, source notes, answer reasoning and [licence notices](LICENSE.md). Our text equivalents support alternate presentation, but your delivery still needs accessibility checking. New accounts, stored work, analytics, live AI or research participation are separate features requiring their own assessment.
