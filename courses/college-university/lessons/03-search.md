# 3. Build a search plan

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Use search suggestions as leads while recording what you actually searched.

## Explanation

A suggested phrase or citation is a lead, not a verified source. For a real assignment, consult your library's search guidance and the relevant database syntax. Our paper exercise uses plain language only; it does not claim to search any live catalogue.

Research searching may need revision as terminology and evidence change. [ACRL's information-literacy framework](../SOURCES.md) discusses purposeful searching; our log and exercises are original teaching tools, not an adaptation of its framework text.

## Worked example

Question: what evidence concerns reading-room conditions and a specified task?

Concept groups: setting—reading room, study space; condition—quiet, noise; outcome—task-completion time, reading comprehension. Do not silently treat completion time and comprehension as identical.

A useful log records question, place searched, date, exact terms, limits, candidate record and why retained or excluded. An assistant suggesting a title does not fill in the “source inspected” field.

## Practice

Use this fictional catalogue:
A. Reading-room pilot; completion times supplied.
B. Campus café preferences; no reading task.
C. Reading-comprehension comparison; methods supplied.
D. Newsletter repeating A.

For a question about comprehension, choose the first record to inspect, one likely exclusion and one caution. Write a paper search-log entry; mark the date and database as simulated.

## Answer and reasoning

Attempt the practice before reading this section.

Inspect C first because its stated outcome is relevant, then check its methods. B is probably outside scope. A measures a different outcome; D repeats A. Example log: “Simulated catalogue; paper activity; terms: reading comprehension + room; retained C for method inspection.” Do not invent a live search date, result count or database access.

## Transfer task

Your first query returns only cafés. Suggest a change without making up new results. One option is to add “reading comprehension” and revise broad “campus space” language. Ask a librarian for field-specific terms if needed. Changing terms is a next step, not evidence that the revised query succeeded.
