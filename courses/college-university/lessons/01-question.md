# 1. Define the question before asking AI

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Turn a broad topic into a question with a clear evidence need.

## Explanation

An AI suggestion can help you think of possibilities, but it does not decide what your assignment asks or establish a research finding. Language-model replies can contain errors even when fluent; [IBM Research](../SOURCES.md) discusses that limitation. You remain responsible for the question, evidence and final argument.

Start with the task, the group or setting, the outcome of interest and the limits. Decide what you could check yourself. Our examples concern a fictional campus reading-room pilot. They are not real studies or recommendations to conduct research.

## Worked example

Broad request: “Prove that quiet rooms make everyone successful.”

Better question: “In the supplied pilot records, what differences in task-completion time were observed between two rooms, and what can the methods support?”

A bounded fictional prompt is: “Suggest questions I should ask about the supplied methods. Do not invent results.” This asks for critique rather than a conclusion. It still requires permission if used in a real assignment.

## Practice

Revise “AI makes university better” into a question answerable from a small supplied source packet. Name one thing the packet would have to measure and one thing it could not establish without further evidence.

## Answer and reasoning

Attempt the practice before reading this section.

One answer: “What do these records report about time taken on a particular reading task?” Completion time would need a definition and measurement. That alone could not establish long-term learning, wellbeing or success across universities. Other well-bounded questions are possible; a narrower question is not automatically a good study design.

## Transfer task

Write a question about a fictional library opening-hours survey. Distinguish satisfaction from actual use. A possible question is “What hours did the respondents say they preferred?” It cannot answer “Which hours improve grades?” unless those outcomes and an appropriate design are available. No actual survey is required.
