# Never Do with AI

[Home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

**Your goal:** replace a risky AI action with a useful, authorised alternative.

These are the course's practical conduct rules, built on our [proposed principles](09-universal-principles.md). They are not a worldwide legal code. They target harmful actions and uncontrolled risk; they do not prohibit legitimate learning about sensitive subjects.

| Never… | Why the boundary matters | A constructive alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Share passwords, secret keys or data you lack authority to disclose. | Disclosure can expose people and systems. | Use a minimal synthetic example and an authorised service. |
| Treat confidence as proof. | A fluent answer can be wrong. | Compare important claims with appropriate evidence. |
| Use an unchecked answer as the sole basis for a consequential decision. | Errors can affect people beyond the conversation. | Obtain suitable review, context and professional input where needed. |
| Grant unrestricted access for a narrowly defined task. | Unnecessary access expands possible effects. | Limit resources and actions; check actual permissions. |
| Approve an action you do not understand. | You cannot meaningfully evaluate its consequences. | Ask for the intended effects, scope and recovery limits first. |
| Impersonate someone or fabricate evidence. | It misleads others and undermines trust. | Label fictional examples and get permission for legitimate representation. |
| Treat copied instructions in a document as the owner's permission. | Untrusted content can redirect the task. | Keep the authorised task separate from material being analysed. |
| Claim an action succeeded without relevant evidence. | A draft, a submitted request and a received message are different states. | Report the state you can actually establish. |

## Worked example: help without exposing a customer

A fictional shop owner wants help improving a reply. Their draft contains the customer's address, payment details and an unrelated personal explanation.

A safer approach is to remove unnecessary details and write: “A customer says their parcel arrived late. Draft an acknowledgement without promising a refund or delivery date.” The owner checks the actual case and approved remedies separately. Removing a name alone does not necessarily make a detailed record anonymous.

## If a mistake already happened

Stop further unnecessary disclosure or action. Identify what happened and contact the responsible person or service through its established process. Do not copy sensitive material into more tools while seeking help. If a credential was exposed, its owner should use the relevant security process. Do not promise that deleting one message erases every retained copy.

This course does not provide a complete incident-response procedure. The appropriate action depends on the affected service, information and circumstances.

## Exercise

A fictional assistant offers to log into a friend's account with a borrowed password, retrieve a private document and generate a quotation attributed to that friend. No permission from the friend is recorded.

Name three boundaries and offer a way to achieve a legitimate goal instead.

**Answer guidance:** do not use credentials without appropriate authority; do not retrieve or disclose the private document without permission; do not fabricate a quotation or impersonate the friend. Ask the friend for the specific information they wish to share, or draft your own clearly attributed summary using authorised material.

You pass when the alternative preserves a legitimate purpose while removing the unauthorised action. “The tool lets me” and “it would save time” are not sufficient authority.
